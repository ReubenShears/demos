# 09 · Ad copy and the ad sheet

## Purpose
Write the text for every ad and assemble the ad sheet. The ad sheet is the single handoff document: Rey builds the ads in Meta from it and nothing else.

## Trigger
Angle plan exists (step 05). Finalise once the creative is approved (step 10).

## The ad sheet

Google Sheet titled `Adset <n> Ad Sheet - <count> Statics`, tab `Static ads`, one row per ad:

| Column | Content |
|--------|---------|
| Ad # | `S1`, `S2` ... matching the file names exactly |
| Angle / Desire | Short phrase. Becomes the quoted part of the ad name |
| Graphic | `In google drive` (the batch folder holds `S<n>` files) |
| Headline | The Meta headline. **Identical on every row** |
| Primary text | Full body copy for that ad |
| Creative notes | What the image shows and why this ad exists in the set |

## Writing the headline
- One headline for the whole ad set: `<Offer> For <ICP>` or `<Callout>? <Offer>.`
- Examples: "Pay Per Booked Job For HVAC Companies Running 3+ Trucks"; "Running DM Ads? Get An AI Setter. Pay Per Shown Call."
- Title case. No results claims.

## Writing the primary text

Structure, in order:

1. **Hook** (1 to 3 short lines), specific to the ad's angle and echoing the image without repeating it word for word.
2. **Turn** (optional, 1 to 3 lines): why the problem exists or what it costs.
3. **Mechanism block**: what the client does and how payment works. **Reuse the same block across the set**, lightly reordered. Example: "We run Google Local Services Ads for your HVAC work, our AI answers and qualifies every call we send, and the job lands booked on your calendar. Installs, repairs, maintenance, year round. You fund the ad spend directly with Google. We only get paid per booked job. No setup fee."
4. **Qualifier**: who it is for, with the size filter. "Built for HVAC companies running 3 or more trucks and doing $1M to $4M."
5. **Call to action** matching the button: "Tap 'Learn more' to see if you're a fit." or "book a discovery call and we'll tell you straight whether your market can support it."

Style:
- Plain, direct, second person. Short paragraphs with blank lines between.
- Arrow bullets (`→`) are fine for three or four benefit lines.
- Length: 60 to 130 words. Minimal ads get 30 to 50.
- Risk reversal stated plainly: "No setup fee. No retainer. If they don't show, you don't pay."
- Revenue or size qualifiers live here, not on the image.
- For a winner variation, model the primary text on the winner's.
- No guarantees of income, no "you" plus personal attributes, no exaggerated claims.

## Description line
One line under the headline, shared by the set: who it is for plus the risk reversal. Example: "For US HVAC companies with install crews. No setup fee, no retainer: pay per booked job."

## Creative notes
Two sentences: the visual, then the reasoning. Example: "Forest green minimalist, type only. The truck filter is the headline so one-truck operators scroll past." These notes are how the next batch learns what was tried.

## Steps
1. Draft all rows from the angle plan.
2. Check every primary text against the offer lock: offer wording, payment terms, geography, size filter.
3. Check each row's Ad # has a matching file and matching Reels-safe file.
4. Share the sheet (anyone with link, viewer) and post it in `#delivery` tagged to Rey (Funnel Architect), with the Drive creative folder link: `ad sheet: <link>` / `ad creative: <link>`.
5. Log the sheet link on the Creative Batch row (step 20).

## Output and handoff
- Ad sheet link and creative folder link in `#delivery`
- Next: step 12, then step 13

## Rules
- Rey asks for the ad sheet by client name each morning. Post it as soon as the client approves; do not wait to be asked.
- If creative is replaced on a live client, the sheet is replaced too ("replace Brett's ads with the new ones, ad sheet: ...").
- Do not change copy after handoff without telling Rey.

## Not confirmed
- Whether more than one primary text or headline per ad is ever used. Live ads inspected have exactly one of each.
