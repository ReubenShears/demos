# 16 · Optimisation and new ad sets

## Purpose
Turn the performance read into the next move: more of what works, less of what does not, and a change of audience or offer when lead quality is the problem.

## Trigger
Day 7 review (step 15), then every week after; or a client report about lead quality or volume.

## Principles in use
1. **Creative is the targeting.** Targeting stays broad. The audience is shaped by who the image and copy call out and by the qualified-only Lead event.
2. **More spend teaches Meta more.** "As spend goes up, we're feeding Meta more pixel data, so it gets better at finding the right people on its own rather than us forcing it into a small pocket."
3. **New creative goes in a new ad set**, numbered next (`Static Adset #2`), inside the same campaign.
4. **Test more angles when volume is low.** "Get lots more angles and creative out to test, as this is the highest probability of getting good lead volume."
5. **Judge on qualified leads and booked calls**, then on shown and closed calls as they come in.

## Decision guide

| Situation | Move |
|-----------|------|
| Clear winners, healthy cost per booked call | Keep running. Draft new ads **off the winners** (variations of angle, format, colour) and launch as a new ad set within the week. Consider raising budget |
| Winners exist but statics are tiring (results falling as the same people see them) | New static ad set from winners **and** start video (step 17) as a further ad set |
| Spend but few qualified leads | Pause the weak ads. New batch with different angles. Check the funnel's qualification rates before blaming the ads |
| Leads qualify on the form but are the wrong businesses | Tighten the **callout on the creative and headline** (e.g. "3+ trucks"), in a new ad set. Tell the client that cost per booked call will rise and close probability should improve. Consider a funnel question change with Reuben |
| Client wants a qualifier on the image | Explain the volume and CPM cost; put it in primary text first |
| Seasonal or regional shift (winter trades, a state to avoid) | Change geography exclusions on a new ad set; add a seasonal angle |
| Offer is not landing | Return to the offer that produced volume; rewrite the offer lock (step 02) and brief a new batch |
| Calls booked but not showing | Not an ads fix. Raise with Reuben and JJ: confirmation page, reminders, qualification |

## Steps
1. Pull performance by ad name for the period (Baserow Ad Data, with attributed leads, bookings, shown calls).
2. Read the client channel for lead-quality feedback since the last review.
3. Choose the move from the guide. Whether a new batch **replaces** the running ad set or runs **alongside** it is Ollie's call.
4. Write the recommendation: what, why, expected effect on cost per booked call, what the client must do (approve creative, record video, confirm budget).
5. Brief step 05 with: winners to vary, angles to drop, new callout, new batch size.
6. When the new ad set is live, pause what it replaces and note the date.
7. Tell the client in one message with dates (step 19).

## Scaling budget
- Raise budget on an ad set that is producing bookings at an acceptable cost; raise in steps, not all at once, and leave it alone for several days after each change.
- Optimally's own account plan is to scale by adding many ad sets by niche and format (image and video sets per audience), then raising total daily spend.

## Output and handoff
- A dated decision per client per week, posted in `#delivery`
- A brief for step 05, or a pause/budget instruction for Rey

## Rules
- Never change more than one major thing at a time on a client (offer, audience callout, funnel questions), or the result cannot be read.
- Do not pause an ad inside its first 7 days except for the faults listed in step 15.
- Record every pause, launch and budget change with its date.

## Not confirmed
- Numeric kill, scale and fatigue thresholds; budget step sizes; frequency caps. Nothing written was found. The table reflects decisions actually made in client channels, not a rulebook. Ask Reuben and Ollie to supply numbers before an agent acts unsupervised.
