The bottleneck

Every decision still routes through you.

If the front desk is silent for four hours, so is the pipeline. If you leave town, the firm stops earning. These are the six leaks that quietly cap most firms at their current number.

Leads sit unanswered

By the time the receptionist calls back, the prospect has hired the next firm on Google. Nine out of ten leads never hear a human voice inside five minutes.

Revenue on a rollercoaster

One month you're booked out; the next, calendars are empty and you're cold-calling old files. The firm reacts to the pipeline instead of the other way around.

Every approval waits on you

Ad copy, intake scripts, refund calls, retainer changes — you're the gatekeeper on all of it. The bar rules are real; the way you clear them isn't scalable.

Ad agencies bill; leads don't book

You're paying a retainer for "traffic" — form fills, unqualified DMs, brochure downloads. Almost none of it turns into a paid consult.

Software you rent, never own

The agency owns the funnel, the tracking, the number, and the reviews. The day you leave, your marketing plumbing walks out of the door with them.

Leaving town is an event

Vacations turn into working weeks; a two-day conference means a Monday of catch-up. The firm can't run for a week without you in the room.

How fast do the leads start showing up?
Consultation-ready leads land inside the first 48 hours of the paid campaigns going live. The intake stack, follow-up, and organic layer stack on top over 90 days so the leads keep coming without lifting the ad spend.

Yes — the qualifier funnel is built for high-ticket legal work: it filters on jurisdiction, budget, and case type before anyone books, so you're not stuck on discovery calls with people looking for free advice. Every campaign is written to your state bar rules and approved by you before it runs.
